You did the right thing — you set up your listing. And yet when customers search, it's your competitors they see. Here are the usual suspects, in the order we check them.

## 1. Your profile isn't verified — or got suspended

An unverified profile is invisible. A suspended one is worse: Google suspends profiles for keyword-stuffed names, address problems, or suspicious edits, often without telling you clearly. Log in and check the banner at the top of your profile first.

## 2. Your category is wrong or too broad

"Contractor" loses to "Emergency plumber" every time someone searches for a burst pipe. Your primary category is the strongest signal you control. Pick the most specific one that matches what you want to be found for, and add secondary categories for the rest.

## 3. Your details disagree across the internet

If your name, address and phone number are listed differently on directories, social profiles and old websites, Google's confidence in your listing drops — and so does your visibility. The details need to match everywhere, exactly. Tedious to fix by hand, which is why it's [part of our service](/services/local-seo/).

## 4. You don't have enough recent reviews

Reviews are votes. A steady drip of recent, replied-to reviews beats a burst from 2022. If asking feels awkward, put a system around it — [here's the routine that works](/learn/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## 5. You're searching from the wrong place

Maps results depend heavily on where the searcher is standing. From your own office you might rank first; three suburbs over, you may not exist. Judge your visibility from the areas you want work in, not from your desk — and if you want customers there, your website needs a page for each of those areas.

## 6. The website behind the listing is weak

Google cross-checks your listing against your website. A slow site that barely mentions what you do or where you do it gives the listing nothing to stand on. That's a [website problem](/services/web-design/), and fixing it lifts the listing too.
