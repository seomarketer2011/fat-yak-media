Speed sounds like a technicality. It's actually one of the most commercial facts about your website. Here's the case, in customer behaviour rather than acronyms.

## The eight-second window

A potential customer taps your site with a decision half-made and patience already thin. Every second of white screen spends it. By three seconds a chunk of them have hit back and tapped the next result; by eight, most have. You don't see any of this happen — no complaint, no bounce notification. Just a phone that rings slightly less than it should.

## Slow is invisible from the inside

You never experience your own site the way customers do. To you it's cached, familiar, on good wi-fi. To a stranger in a car park on two bars of signal, it's a cold start. This is why owners are so often shocked the first time they watch their site load on someone else's phone — and it's the test worth running today.

## Google is watching the same thing

When searchers repeatedly tap a result, wait, give up and come back, Google reads it exactly as you would: this page disappoints people. Slow sites drift down; the businesses above you inherit your calls. Speed isn't a separate issue from [showing up on Google](/learn/why-your-website-doesnt-show-up-on-google/) — it's one of its quiet foundations.

## Why most sites are slow

Not malice — accumulation. A heavy theme with a drag-and-drop builder, a dozen plugins each adding their bit, photos uploaded straight off a phone at full size, cheap hosting. Each choice was convenient; the stack is a barge. That's also why "make it faster" retrofits disappoint: you're sanding a barge.

## What fast means, concretely

The page appears in about a second on a phone. The number is tappable immediately. Nothing shifts around as things load.
