Most small-business websites fail the same way: built once, built pretty, built without a job description. Here's the job description — seven things, in priority order.

## 1. It loads before they give up

Speed comes first because nothing else gets seen without it. Every second of loading bleeds visitors back to Google. This one matters enough that [it has its own guide](/learn/why-website-speed-wins-customers/).

## 2. The first screen answers three questions

What do you do, where do you do it, and what should I tap next. A visitor should know all three without scrolling: "Emergency plumbing across Northside — Call now." If your first screen is a slideshow and a welcome message, you're paying for visitors and showing them a riddle.

## 3. The phone number is always one thumb away

On mobile — where most of your visitors are — a tap-to-call button should follow the visitor down every page. Every extra step between "I'm convinced" and "it's ringing" loses real jobs.

## 4. Proof beats promises

Reviews pulled from Google, photos of actual jobs, the real team, recognisable local landmarks. Visitors have seen ten thousand stock photos; they trust none of them. Your slightly imperfect photo of Tuesday's finished job is worth more than any of it.

## 5. A page for every service

One "Our Services" page listing nine things ranks for none of them. A dedicated page for each service — what it costs, how it works, photos, questions answered — can rank for each. This is the difference between a brochure and [a site that gets found](/learn/why-your-website-doesnt-show-up-on-google/).

## 6. A page for every area

Same logic, geographic edition. Google matches searches like "electrician Northside" to pages about electrical work *in Northside*. No page, no match. Genuine local detail — jobs done there, landmarks, service notes — keeps these pages useful rather than wallpaper.

## 7. A contact form with no homework

Name, contact, "what do you need" — three fields. Every additional field costs conversions. And answer fast: the business that replies within the hour usually takes the job.

## What you can skip

Animations, sliders, background video, award badges nobody's heard of, and anything described as "digital experience". They cost speed (see #1) and add nothing a customer cares about.
