Getting on Google Maps is one of the highest-value twenty-minute jobs a local business owner can do. Here is the whole process, without the jargon.

## Step 1: Claim your free Google Business Profile

Go to [google.com/business](https://www.google.com/business/) and sign in with a Google account. Search for your business first — Google often creates unclaimed listings from public records, and claiming an existing one is better than creating a duplicate.

## Step 2: Enter your details exactly, once, properly

Use your real business name — not "Smith Plumbing | Best Plumber Northside 24/7". Google suspends profiles for keyword-stuffed names. Pick the most specific category that describes what you do; it's the strongest signal you control. If customers come to you, enter your address. If you go to them, choose a service-area business and list the suburbs you cover.

## Step 3: Verify that you own the business

Google needs proof you're really there. Depending on your business type it will offer verification by postcard, phone, email or — increasingly — a short video walkthrough of your premises or work vehicle. Video is usually fastest. Until you verify, your edits stay invisible.

## Step 4: Fill in everything, then keep it alive

Opening hours, phone number, website, services, and at least a dozen real photos of your work, your team and your premises. A complete profile isn't box-ticking: profiles with photos and fresh activity earn dramatically more calls than skeletons. Post an update every couple of weeks, even a small one — Google notices signs of life, and so do customers.

## Step 5: Start collecting reviews immediately

Reviews are the fuel of Maps visibility. Ask every happy customer, make it effortless with a direct link or QR code, and reply to every review you get. Our guide to [getting more Google reviews](/learn/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) covers the exact routine.

## What being listed doesn't get you

A listing makes you *eligible* to appear — it doesn't make you *chosen*. Whether you crack the three spots shown on the map depends on reviews, relevance, your website and how close the searcher is.
