"We have a website, but nobody finds it" is the most common sentence we hear. There are only three underlying causes. Every invisible website has at least one of them.

## Cause 1: Google can't find it

Google discovers pages by following links. A brand-new site that nothing links to is an island — Google may simply not have arrived yet. New sites also start with zero track record, so even once found, they surface slowly. The fix is patience plus signals: get listed in the places that matter ([your Google Business Profile](/learn/how-to-get-your-business-on-google-maps/) first), and earn mentions from local sites Google already trusts.

## Cause 2: Google can't read it

Some sites accidentally tell Google to go away — a leftover "noindex" setting from the build, a blocked robots file, pages that take eight seconds to load, or text buried inside images and animations Google can't parse. These are silent killers: the site looks fine to you and blank to Google. A technical check-up finds them in an afternoon, and fixing them is usually quick. This is the "fix the boat before rowing harder" step of [our approach](/services/seo/).

## Cause 3: Google doesn't trust it — yet

The most common case: the site is indexed, readable… and outcompeted. Google ranks pages, not businesses. If there's no page on your site that squarely answers "emergency electrician in Northside", Google has nothing to show for that search — so it shows your competitor, who wrote one. Trust is earned page by page: cover the questions your customers actually ask, in plain language, on fast pages, and collect mentions and reviews that vouch for you.

## How to tell which cause is yours

One minute of diagnosis: search `site:yourwebsite.com`. Nothing? You have cause 1 or 2 — a findability problem. Pages appear but rankings don't? Cause 3 — a competition problem. The treatments are completely different, which is why guessing wastes money.
