A new customer journey is quietly appearing: instead of searching and scrolling, people ask an assistant — and it answers with names. Here's what's actually happening under the hood, minus the mysticism.

## The new front door

Ask ChatGPT "who's a good physio in Northside?" and you don't get ten blue links — you get two or three named clinics with a sentence about each. Google now does something similar with AI-generated answers above its own results, and voice assistants have been doing it for years ("call the nearest locksmith"). Different systems, same shift: **the assistant narrows the field before the customer ever sees a list.**

## Where the answers come from

No secret database. Assistants pull from what's publicly written about you: your Google Business Profile and its reviews, directories, local articles that mention you, and your own website. Modern assistants search the live web mid-conversation, then summarise what they find. The assistant is essentially a very fast reader of your public reputation.

## Why consistency suddenly matters more

A human sifting messy sources brings patience; software doesn't. If your name is styled three ways across the web, your hours disagree, or your site never plainly says what you do and where, an assistant reading at speed can't confidently stitch you together — so it names the tidy competitor it *can*. The old advice about [consistent details everywhere](/learn/why-your-business-doesnt-show-up-on-google-maps/) went from bookkeeping to entry ticket.

## Fewer winners per question

A results page has room for ads, a map, ten links — many businesses get some scrap of visibility. An AI answer names one to three. That's a harsher cut, and it compounds: assistants tend to repeat well-evidenced businesses. Early movers are writing themselves into the answers while competitors debate whether it matters.

## The reassuring part

None of this requires abandoning what works. The evidence AI reads is what Google already rewards: a complete profile, [steady reviews](/learn/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/), a clear fast website, consistent mentions. One body of work, two channels.
